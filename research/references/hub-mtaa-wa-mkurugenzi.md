# Hub: Mtaa wa Mkurugenzi (reference)

Inspired by Kibera and Eastlands. Where no measured source exists, sizes are **estimates** scaled from a 1.75 m person and a 2.1 x 0.9 m door, and are marked (est).

## 1. Real-world references
- Kibera informal settlement (Nairobi). Toi Market on Suna Road: open-air, about 3 acres, a "labyrinth of stalls" (clothes, food, hardware). Fires in 2008 and 2019; traders rebuilt with donated iron sheets, so expect **mixed old rusted and newer shiny mabati**.
- The Kibera railway line: residents walk along the tracks; a raised embankment on the old colonial line gives a view over the rooftops. Kenya Railways evicts structures within the rail reserve, so the strip next to the tracks is a **cleared, beaten-earth corridor with stalls creeping back**.
- Eastlands two-to-four-storey block flats (Umoja/Kayole style): walk-ups with balconies and washing lines.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toi_Market · https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/73619-nairobi-traders-abandon-kenya-railways-multi-million-facility · https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/corporate/tech/How-slum-fire-sparked-idea-of-safe-homes/4258474-5076800-11g4o6g/index.html · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibera

## 3. Materials and ageing
- **Mabati:** corrugated galvanised iron (about 28 to 32 gauge), 76 mm pitch corrugation. Ages from silver to dull grey, then orange rust from the bottom edge and nail holes. Patched sheets in different shades. Roofs weighed down with stones, tyres and bricks.
- **Mud and wattle:** a timber pole frame filled with mud, sometimes cement-plastered and painted. Plaster cracks and falls away at the base, showing the brown-red mud.
- **Concrete block** (block flats, church, shops): rendered and painted, with damp stains and red dust splash up to 40 cm.
- **Timber:** rough-sawn doors, often painted blue or green, faded and peeling.
- Ground: red murram, compacted and rutted, with plastic litter and puddles after rain.

## 5. Signage
- Hand-painted with a brush in block capitals, sometimes with drop shadows, uneven letter spacing and phone numbers.
- Mixed English/Swahili/Sheng: "HOTELI", "CHIPS FRESH", "KINYOZI" (barber), "BUTCHERY: NYAMA CHOMA", "HARDWARE", "MITUMBA", "VIDEO SHOW LEO 7PM", "WAKARIBISHWA".
- Barbershop boards with painted haircut menus ("Bob", "Taper", "Chiskop").
- Fictional brands only (see style-guide.md).

## 6. Life and clutter
People: hawkers, mama mboga, kids in school uniform or playing football, boda riders waiting at the stage, mkokoteni pushers, men on benches at the barber.
Objects: plastic chairs, jerricans (yellow 20 L), charcoal jikos with smoke, sacks of charcoal, soda crates, black plastic water tanks (500 to 1,000 L), washing lines, satellite dishes, tangled wires, plank bridges over drains, posters for crusades and concerts.
Animals: chickens, stray dogs, cats, the odd goat.

## 7. Time of day
- **06:00 to 08:00:** cool mist, smoke from jikos, people streaming to the road, mandazi and chai stalls.
- **Midday:** harsh near-vertical sun, short shadows, dust, quieter lanes.
- **17:00 to 19:00:** peak bustle, golden red-dust haze, music from shops, kids playing.
- **Night:** sparse orange street light, single bulbs over kiosks, TV glow from the video hall, dogs barking.
